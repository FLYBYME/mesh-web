PART 2 — ONE REGISTRATION POINT, SO `StorageProvider` STOPS BEING A SPECIAL CASE

Today there are two driver mechanisms and they do not know about each other:

- `StorageProvider` — an ops table, several implementations, bound per hive, with
  `RESOLUTION_ORDER` as the search path. A VFS borrowed from NT deliberately, and
  `src/kernel/hives.ts` says so.
- `RENDERER` — a provider token, resolved through the object registry.

The roadmap's own words: *"The shape to copy is NT's I/O manager, and half of it
is already here. The kernel has an object registry with tokens, a configuration
manager with hives, and a capability set that is the personality a part programs
against — `needs(...)` is the syscall surface. What it lacks is the I/O manager:
**one place a driver registers, one interface per subsystem, resolved at load.**"*

Build that. Both the renderer and storage register through it; nothing else
changes shape. The test that this is real: **a driver registered for a subsystem
that already has one is refused, or replaces it deliberately — say which and
test it.** Two drivers silently both installed is the kind of thing discovered
months later by a bug that makes no sense.

Do **not** turn this into a rewrite of hives. `StorageProvider`'s resolution
order is a decided design and it works; this is about where a driver is
*registered*, not how storage resolves a path.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3 — THE DRIVERS THAT ARE MISSING, DRIVEN BY WHAT ASKED

Only build what something asks for. The roadmap names the asks:

- **a window driver** — `window.open` and cross-origin messages, which a hosted
  sign-in wants
- **online and offline** — `navigator.onLine`, and the events

The evidence that this gap is real, and the shape of it: *"a part that wants one
has nothing to declare and nothing to ask for, which is how `sessionTicketStore`
came to read `globalThis.sessionStorage` — **a part with nowhere to ask**, when
the `session` hive was already the driver it wanted."*

For each driver you add, the same two things or do not add it:

1. a part can **declare** it needs the subsystem and be refused at load if no
   driver is registered — not fail at the moment it calls
2. a **fake** implementation in a test that proves the seam, the way the
   recording renderer does — if you cannot write the fake, the interface is
   shaped around the browser and has not been separated from it

`matchMedia`, the clipboard, notifications and visibility are named in the
roadmap as having no seam either. **Do not add them speculatively.** A driver
nothing asks for is an interface nobody has tested against a second
implementation, which is the whole finding this item came from.

WHAT NOT TO DO

- Do not touch `@flybyme/mesh` (frozen).
- Do not edit mesh-core, mesh-serve or mesh-operator. Separate repositories, and
  dispatches may be running in them. If you need something from one, write it
  into `spec/roadmap.md` with its evidence and work around it here.
- `as any` and `as never` are bugs here, not style nits. Two dispatches shipped
  casts today and both were taken back out within the hour — one of them was in
  the swap test for part (1), reaching a button through `card.view() as any`. If
  you need a cast you have the wrong shape; say so in the report.
- Do not assert that a token was registered and call it a test. That is the exact
  mistake part (1)'s first test made: it proves the registry works and says
  nothing about the driver being swappable.
